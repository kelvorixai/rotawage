National Bonds vs. fixed deposits: how two popular UAE savings vehicles actually differ

Both get mentioned as "safe places to park savings" in the same conversation. Structurally, one is a profit-sharing scheme with prize draws and no guaranteed rate; the other is a guaranteed, fixed-rate bank product. They're not substitutes for the same job.

National Bonds and a standard bank fixed (term) deposit both get recommended as low-drama places to hold savings — and both are, in the sense that neither involves picking stocks or timing markets. But the mechanism behind each one is genuinely different, and that difference matters more than the headline numbers suggest.

How each one actually pays you

A bank fixed deposit is straightforward: you lock a sum for a set term, the bank quotes a fixed interest rate up front, and — barring an early withdrawal — you know exactly what you'll get at maturity. The rate is guaranteed by the bank for the term you agreed to.

National Bonds works on a different structure entirely — a Mudarabah, an Islamic profit-sharing partnership rather than an interest-bearing account. Your money goes into a diversified portfolio (real estate, sukuk, Sharia-screened equities, money-market instruments), and a profit rate is declared annually based on how that portfolio actually performed. There's no fixed rate promised in advance, and the principal isn't formally guaranteed the way a bank deposit's is, even though the product is run with a strong emphasis on capital preservation. Declared annual rates have varied over time — recent years have landed in the roughly 2.5%–5% range — which is the trade-off for not locking in a fixed number up front.

Liquidity: both are more flexible than they sound

National Bonds' standard savings product allows withdrawal after a short minimum holding period — commonly around 30 days for cash or cheque contributions, longer for credit-card contributions — with smaller amounts available near-instantly through the provider's app or partner exchanges, and larger amounts via bank transfer over a few working days. Fixed deposits are the more rigid of the two in this respect: withdrawing before the agreed term typically comes with an early-withdrawal penalty that eats into or eliminates the interest earned, since the bank priced the rate assuming the full term.

The prize-draw layer

National Bonds also runs a rewards programme funded separately from the profit-share — every unit of savings held generates entries into periodic prize draws, with prizes that can include large cash sums. This is a genuine feature of the product, but it's explicitly not guaranteed income — it's a lottery-style bonus layered on top of the profit-share, not a rate you can plan around. Treating expected prize value as part of your return is the same mistake as counting on any other low-probability payout.

A fixed deposit suits money you want a known, contractual number against — a specific amount by a specific date. National Bonds suits money you're comfortable seeing grow at a variable rate in exchange for Sharia compliance, more flexible access, and the optionality of the prize programme. Neither is strictly "better" — they're solving for different things, and the honest comparison is about which trade-off matches what the money is actually for.
