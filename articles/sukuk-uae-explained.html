Sukuk in the UAE: what the lock-in period actually costs you

A fixed-term sukuk often quotes a better rate than a liquid alternative. The lock-in period is the part worth pricing in before deciding that's actually the better deal.

Sukuk are Islamic finance's answer to a conventional bond — instead of paying interest, a sukuk represents partial ownership in an underlying asset or venture, with returns structured as profit shares rather than interest payments, keeping it compliant with Sharia principles that prohibit interest (riba). For a UAE-based saver, they're a common option alongside conventional fixed-income products, and issuers regularly offer multi-year terms — two years is a common structure — at rates that look meaningfully better than what a liquid savings product offers.

The trade that's easy to skip past

A better headline rate is real, but it's not the whole comparison. The rate is compensation for two things bundled together: the credit/structure risk of the specific sukuk, and the liquidity you give up for the term. A liquid alternative — a flexible savings account, a money-market fund, an accumulation fund you can redeem without penalty — pays less specifically because you can access it at any time. That access has value, and it's easy to underweight it when comparing headline rates side by side.

What "locked in" actually means in practice

During the term, the funds generally aren't available without penalty or aren't available at all, depending on the specific structure — some products allow early redemption at a discount, others don't allow it at all. Two years is long enough that circumstances can change in ways worth planning for in advance: a job change, relocation, an unplanned expense, a better opportunity appearing mid-term. None of these are hypothetical for anyone on a contract-based or rotational career — contract renewals, site reassignments, and employer changes are exactly the kind of thing that can collide badly with a two-year lock-in.

A simple way to price the trade-off

FactorSukuk (locked-term)Liquid alternative
Typical headline rateHigherLower
Access to funds during termRestricted or penalizedAvailable anytime
Best fit forMoney you're confident you won't need for the full termMoney that needs to stay flexible

A useful rule of thumb: only commit an amount to a fixed-term product that you could genuinely leave untouched even in a worse-than-expected scenario — a gap between contracts, a larger-than-planned expense, a change of location. Everything else belongs in something liquid, even at a lower quoted rate, because the value of being able to access it isn't visible in the rate itself.

The actual question to ask before committing

Not "which rate is higher" but "what does it cost me if I need this money before the term ends, and how likely is that, realistically, given my own situation over the next two years." For most people the honest answer isn't zero — and once it's priced in, the comparison often looks closer than the headline rates alone suggest.
