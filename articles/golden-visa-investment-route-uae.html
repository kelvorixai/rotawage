The UAE Golden Visa investment route: what each path actually requires

"Invest AED 2 million, get a 10-year visa" is the headline version. There are actually four distinct investment-based routes with different thresholds, different durations, and different fine print — here's what each one actually asks for.

The UAE's Golden Visa is a long-term residency permit — typically 5 or 10 years, renewable, and not tied to a local sponsor or employer the way a standard work visa is. It covers several eligibility categories (specialized talent, outstanding students, and others), but the routes most relevant to someone building savings or running a business are the investment-based ones. There are four of them, and they're often talked about as if there's just one AED 2 million property rule — there isn't.

The four investment-based routes

Each route has its own threshold, its own duration, and its own conditions on how the money has to be held. The property route is the one most commonly discussed, but it isn't the only door in, and it isn't necessarily the cheapest one relative to what it buys.

The property route, in more detail

Because it's the most commonly used entry point, it's worth being precise about how the AED 2 million threshold is actually measured. A fully-owned property valued at or above the threshold qualifies outright. A mortgaged property also qualifies — but on your equity, not the property's total value, so a mortgaged property needs your paid-in portion (down payment plus principal repaid) to reach AED 2 million, not just the purchase price. Off-plan property typically needs a completion certificate and, depending on the case, developer-level approval before it counts. Joint ownership is prorated — each named owner generally needs their proportionate share to independently clear the threshold.

What the visa does — and doesn't — buy you

A Golden Visa removes the need for a local sponsor or employer to hold your residency, and it's renewable as long as the qualifying investment is maintained. It does not, by itself, grant citizenship, and it doesn't remove the requirement to hold a valid Emirates ID or otherwise meet standard residency administration. Disposing of the qualifying asset before renewal is generally treated as breaking eligibility for the category — the investment isn't a one-time ticket, it's closer to a standing condition of the visa.

The real question before committing capital

The visa itself has real value for anyone planning to stay in the UAE long-term — sponsorship independence and multi-year stability aren't nothing. But AED 2 million (or AED 10 million, on the large-scale route) is also a significant sum that could otherwise be invested more liquidly, at a return you control, without being tied to a single asset or jurisdiction. The honest framing isn't "is the Golden Visa worth it" in the abstract — it's "is locking up this specific amount, in this specific asset, worth more to me than what that same capital could otherwise earn or how liquid I'd otherwise want it to stay." That's a personal calculation, not a universal one, and it's worth running with real numbers before treating the visa as a foregone conclusion.
